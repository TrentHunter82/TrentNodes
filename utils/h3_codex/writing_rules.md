# Trent H3 writing rules

Apply these creative requirements alongside the official MiniMax format. Keep
the official section names, reference syntax, shot markers and sound notation.

## Finished appearance and consistent identity

Describe finished materials, lighting, composition and movement. Do not describe
the source or result as grayscale, sketchy or drawn. Extract identity and anatomy
from the connected references, and finished surface and lighting cues from the
connected finished renders or the user's stated target look.

Every time you mention a subject, character or visual reference label, repeat
its relevant concrete, positive appearance description, not just its name,
pronoun or label. Carry the SAME identity traits from subject definitions and
retention analysis into the video description and every later mention. This
applies to unnamed animals, ships, environments and other subjects too. Ground
these traits in the actual connected references; do not invent or change them
between sections. If a needed trait is not visible or supplied, keep it
unspecified and report the uncertainty in assumptions.

## Reference roles

Give each connected reference a specific role, using the supplied reference_roles
and visible evidence. Character sheets establish identity and anatomy. Connected
finished renders establish materials, lighting and rendering style. Each cut's
source frames establish pose, framing, action and timing. A finished render does
not replace a character sheet's identity or anatomy. Do not transfer turnaround
poses, sheet layouts or unrelated reference scenery into the target scene.
Explain conflicting or unclear reference roles in assumptions rather than
silently changing identity. Do not require or invent separately supplied styled
opening frames. Use the references that are actually connected.

## Shot timing and action

When shot_timeline is nonempty, use exactly those shots in that order. Preserve
the cut times on the original timeline; do not retime, merge or add cuts. Write
the supplied prompt_marker for each shot in the video description. The first
shot uses the official untimed [Shot 1] marker; subsequent markers include their
exact cut time. Keep the official transition wording and describe any supplied
transition kind in the action. End each shot at its supplied end time.

For every shot, explicitly describe its opening pose and composition, when
movement begins, intermediate actions, camera movement, and ending pose. Use
absolute clip times for action beats and the ending pose, within that shot's
start/end interval. Distinguish swimming from standing, looking from turning,
and camera movement from subject movement. "Follow the source" alone is not
sufficient. If there is no movement, describe the held pose and static camera
when supported by the evidence.

Use each shot's timestamped source samples for timing and visible state changes.
Sparse stills do not establish an exact movement onset or everything between
samples: identify estimated timing or missing evidence in assumptions, and do
not claim to observe unsampled motion. Cut times alone establish structure,
not action; without source frames, use the supplied brief/context and distinguish
creative timing choices from observed timing in assumptions.

## Space and visibility

State what occupies the foreground, middle distance and background, which way
subjects face and travel, what is offscreen, and what is partially obscured.
Preserve intentional occlusion and the source composition. Describe only the
visible or supplied traits of hidden subjects rather than revealing or inventing
occluded anatomy or scenery.

Before returning, review every shot against its source samples, reference roles
and timing, then check every subject mention for the same grounded appearance
traits throughout all sections. Keep uncertainty in assumptions, outside the
finished H3 prompt.
